AgentGuard

Make agent tool calls subject to explicit policies, limits and inspectable audit records.

Reference service Policy engine Hash-linked audit Next branch
01 / Project focus

Boundaries before
capability.

AgentGuard decides whether a specific tool call is permitted, under which limits, and records the reasoning so the decision can be questioned later.

Policy evaluation

Each tool call is evaluated against a declared policy: role, tool, resource scope and rate limit. A call that matches no rule is denied rather than permitted by default.

Rate-limiting boundaries

Limits are enforced per caller and per tool. Exceeding a boundary produces a refusal with the limit that was hit, not a timeout or an empty result.

Hash-linked audit

Decisions are appended to an audit chain where each entry includes the hash of the previous one. A modified or removed entry breaks verification of everything after it.

Human review

Approval requirements are part of the policy, not a separate workflow. A rule can require an explicit human decision for a specific class of call.

03 / Scope and verification

What this does
not claim.

Boundaries matter more than endorsements when you are deciding whether to run a system against your own tools.

Rate limits and audit scope

Rate limits and audit scope depend on the implementation and deployment. A demonstration endpoint does not establish production security.

Source and publication

Source availability and publication records do not certify the current Next revision. Review release notes, tests and deployment assumptions for your environment.
